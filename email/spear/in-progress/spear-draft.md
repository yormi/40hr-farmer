I started Orisha because I wanted to help small growers. I didn't know anything about agriculture but helping farmers feed the world seemed a noble cause to work for.    
A lot of farmers and greenhouse consultants were kind enough to let me pick their brain. I wanted to understand how to get the most out of a greenhouse.

Quickly, I realized how much work it was. Trying to reconcile all the rule of thumbs, the instinct,
the weather, the plant stage and so on.

I consider myself well-verse in physics, science, programming but trying to
develop a greenhouse controller was a nightmare. You needed to reprogram
everything everyday. The temperature you need in the greenhouse depends on how
much sunlight the plant receive. So every week, the ideal conditions change. Same for irrigation. Then, the weather
impacts when to open the sides. The power of the heater changes is important to
not have the ventilation fight it and so on. It was a mess and I was working
full time on it. No plant, no employees or other responsibility to tend to and
it was a mess. No way every farmer needs to figure that out. We need to find a
simpler way to do it. No shock there. That's basically what everyone was asking.

That's how Orisha built a greenhouse controller that would serve farmers coached
by greenhouse consultants. The majority of small farmers were working with a
greenhouse consultant. Thanks for the subsidies Quebec Agricultural Ministry !

2 years later, most small growers in Quebec were working with us. So, we thought
we spent so much time working on the greenhouse controller, we should make it
available to more growers.

"You gotta realize it's intimidating for us", Luke from Terramore Farm told us.

It took us time to realize that farmers outside of Quebec don't have the same subsidized support we have in Quebec. Everybody were asking "what temperature should I keep in the greenhouse", "What kind of irrigation cycle should I aim for ?". We used to let the greenhouse consultant take care of that.

We start to speak with growers outside of Quebec and people ask us what temperature they need in their high tunnels, what kind of irrigation cycle to aim for and so on. Where are their greenhouse consultants ?

We give tips over email, build a tomato course, make Orisha smarter to get the expertise on the farm at a fraction of a consultant costs. During that time, we receive questions that cover so many edge case we couldn't plan for. Every farm is different, they say...

I believe running a small farm profitably can be done on a 40hr week.

There're a lot of challenges and though calls to make to get there though.

I've worked alongside market gardeners for almost 10 years now and keep hearing the same story from every farmers :
1. Start a farm to build a better future for the planet, the community and ourselves
2. Face financial pressure, scale the farm to increase sales
3. Expenses hike faster than sales
4. Bridge the gap with our own free labor
5. The hours crush us

Yet, very few farmers continue the story with how they got out of that trap.

Those who did, all used the same rough playbook : hunt every opportunity to do more with less.

Easier said than done, right ?

Well, recently, something clicked ! What we've been working on for years is probably the greatest opportunity to do more with less.

The gap between the best small-scale greenhouse growers and the average grower is huge. And everything lead me to believe we can bridge that gap.

A greenhouse bed that brings what 20 field beds bring change the possibilities for small farms. It gives us so much more power to reinvent our farm in a healthier way.

Greenhouse consultants help small growers in Quebec get there. But doing it without their experience is quite an ask.

I was wondering how Orisha could best support farmers to do it, when Andrew Mefferd offered to partner on a video course. Talking it through, we landed somewhere bigger: videos alone are not enough, better tools either, and we definitely don't have the capacity to help everyone through coaching. Maybe a mix would work ?

That's how The 40hr Farmer Program is born.

We definitely don't have it all figured out but it surely needs to start
somewhere and it'll get better as we go! Slowly but surely paving the way to
doing more with less. Starting with greenhouse tomatoes.

Last year, I started a farm to test the do-more-with-less ideas I've gathered over the years. The initial plan : $700,000 with 2 employees. Greenhouse tomatoes are at the center of that plan.

One year in, the plan has change countless times. Next email : What's actually happening.
