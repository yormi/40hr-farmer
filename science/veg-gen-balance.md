# Veg/gen balance — how sugar picks its sink


## Model


### Phloem as a pull system


- sugar flows in the phloem down a pressure gradient → toward whichever sink unloads it fastest

- unloading speed = **sink strength**   (the fastest unloader wins the flow; sinks compete, sugar is not rationed)


### Two kinds of sink


- **shoot** (head, suckers) → growth sink: sugar spent building new cells (division, expansion, walls, protein)

- **fruit** → storage sink: sugar unloaded, split, stashed as sugars and starch in cells that already exist

- fruit unloading keeps sugar concentration at the fruit end low → gradient stays pointed at the fruit


### Temperature asymmetry


- growth-sink pull → steeply temperature-sensitive   (building tissue stalls in cool nights)

- storage-sink pull → weakly temperature-sensitive   (stashing barely slows)

- cool night → shoot pull collapses first, fruit pull barely drops → fruit's **share** of the pool rises

- cold speeds nothing up; everything slows, fruit slows least   (generative steering starves the competitor, it does not feed the fruit)

- warm night → shoot pulls all night AND respiration burns pool   (flat day/night profile → vegetative; see [`climate.md`](climate.md))
