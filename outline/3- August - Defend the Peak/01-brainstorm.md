# Month 3: Fight off diseases

Status: locked 2026-06-29 (Stage 1 brainstorm, Guillaume-approved).
Source for the Google Doc Brainstorm tab.

## Month goal

- **Protect peak-season yield through August**, keeping the fruit-loaded
  plant healthy and producing through humidity and disease pressure instead
  of losing clusters to bacterial spot, powdery mildew, botrytis, leaf mold and
  so on.

## Stakes of failing

August is the bed running at full output: the clusters set in July are
filling and ripening all at once. It is also when heat, humidity, and
disease peak together. 

- **Disease wiping out filled fruit.** Bacterial spot, leaf mold, and gray
  mold move fast in a dense, humid August canopy and take fruit that
  already cost a full season to grow. Gordon reached the point of
  questioning whether maintenance was worth it and considering letting the
  plants go.

## Actionables

### Disease scouting

- **Keep the plant vigorous:** a strong, balanced plant shrugs off disease;
  a stressed or stalled one feeds it. The Month 2 vigor work is the first
  line of defense.
- **Feed calcium, watch deficiency:** calcium-short fruit and tissue turn
  fragile and botrytis-prone. Story at Ferme Decembre.
- **Spray preventively on a schedule?:** biofungicide (Serenade) → dial the
  trigger, frequency, and dose before disease takes hold, not after. (Sulfur Andrew?)
- **Run a sanitation protocol:** disinfect tools and hands, clear culls and
  dead tissue out of the tunnel, don't carry spores plant to plant. (What else
  Antoine ?)
- **Weekly deep inspection:** one person walks every path both ways, bends
  under leaves, spot-checks clusters → white spots, yellow leaves, wilting,
  gray moss, condensation, puddles.
- **Train the crew to spot disease:** color photo of the main diseases and
  pests at eye level in the tunnel.

### Airflow

- **Remove dead material as you prune:** container ahead of you, drop every
  cut leaf and cluster, empty outside the tunnel at row end.
- **Run airflow without heat:** ducted heater fan on the poly-tube to break
  up humidity pooling at the plant base; off when humidity drops.
- **Pulse the air on humid spells:** roll-ups and heater wide open ~6 min,
  repeat every 40 min while humidity stays high.
- **Variety holds or folds under disease:** Gordon's Cuba Libre fended off
  bacterial spot best, Geronimo worst. Note for next year's seed order.

### Humidity control

- **Power-vent humid mornings:** vent point 1 to 2F below the heater setting
  to expel warm wet air, warm the cool intake → no condensation.
- **Keep RH under 80%:** above it transpiration stalls and disease seeds.
- **Ramp morning temp in stages:** open roll-ups in steps following the
  temperature, never wide open at sunrise → no condensation on cold fruit.
- **Anti-condensation treatment on the plastic:** if not replacing it soon,
  cuts the drip that seeds disease and steals light.
- **Heat to dehumidify, even on warm days:** run the heater with vents
  cracked to drive moisture out; warm wet air holds more water than cool dry
  air, so a warm day can still be a humid one.
- **Get a humidity sensor:** current and overnight humidity graph; can't
  manage August humidity by gut.

### Fruit-load

- **Pull immature clusters to rescue a weak plant:** costs short-term yield, saves the plant.

### Irrigation

- **Stop water ~2 h before sunset, start ~2 h after sunrise:** no standing
  water overnight → reduce root disease issues

### Canopy hygiene

- Remove infected flower, fruit, clusters. Carve out botrytis from the stem and spray (what do you
  recommend guys ?).
- **Prune weekly to keep foliar area:** unpruned → dead airflow, disease.
- **Prune below ripening fruit:** airflow up, disease down, ripening faster.
- **Prune in the morning, plants turgid:** clean snaps; afternoon tissue
  mashes.
- Prune leaves with 1/3 covered by deficiency decoloration before they open the
  door to diseases
- **No stubs:** snap tight to the stem or cut to living tissue → stubs rot
  back and infect.
- **Snap by hand or disinfect between plants:** a blade carries disease
  plant to plant.

## Stories

- **Gordon, bacterial spot on green fruit.** "So far, it's been the
  rainiest and most humid summer on record, so the disease isn't a big
  surprise." Geronimo hit worst, Cuba Libre fending it off best; leaf-pruned
  hard and sprayed Serenade.
- **Drew, condensation seeds leaf mold.** Anti-condensate plastic still
  dripping in cold-outside, warm-humid-inside spells; pruned lower and inner
  leaves harder in response.
- **Drew, the August crunch.** A record market week pulled labor toward
  salad, brassica, and peas while cherry tomatoes fell from 140 to 80 lb in a
  week and cucumbers from 361 to 160. August is exactly when disease scouting
  and maintenance are most likely to slip, because the hours they need go to
  the competing harvests.
