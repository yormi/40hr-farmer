# Heat — the last resort

Greenhouse tomatoes. What to do when the house is **still too
hot** after everything else is dialed in.

**Precondition:** reach for this only after every other card —
irrigation, pruning, fruit load, feeding, HAF — is already
tuned. Healthy plants tolerate far more heat than growers
think. The big greenhouse-tomato regions are hot Spain and
Mexico.


## The plant's own AC

- Healthy big leaves with enough water transpire ~10x more and
  cool the canopy up to **~15°F**.
- Each bed of healthy transpiring leaves cools like two big
  window ACs.
- If irrigation can't supply the water, transpiration stops and
  you lose **all** of it. Water supply is the real lever.


## Lever 1 — reduce fruit load

Fewer fruit = more sugar for growth. Every fruit dropped lets
the plant tolerate **~2°F** warmer before vigor breaks.

- **Sales pause coming** → prune a whole cluster **~7 weeks**
  ahead.
- **Otherwise** → lower fruit per leader **evenly**: new
  clusters to **2.5 fruit** (alternate 3, 2 for consistency).
- **Vigor still not recovering** → **2 fruit**.
- **Quick fix** → pull from older clusters not yet at full-size
  fruit.
- See `playbook/fruit-load/fruit-thinning.md`.


## Lever 2 — shade, last

- **1% light lost ≈ 1% yield lost.**
- Shade blocks light 100% of the time, even when it's not sunny
  → less sugar for the same fruit load → less vigor.
- Only helps when roots genuinely can't supply water fast
  enough for transpiration.


## Experimental / unproven

Theory / untested — do not run as locked protocol.

- **30% diffusing shade** — theory says ~5°F cooler under July
  sun; the real win is cutting the plant's water demand.
- **Night window-AC idea** — worth exploring, not tested. Close
  the sides, run a 25,000 BTU window AC at **night** (night
  matters most: respiration vs photosynthesis). Theory ~5°F
  drop; two units ~10°F; ~$1/night of electricity per °F. Watch
  humidity.
