# Brainstorm file spec (`01-brainstorm.md`)

## Structure
1. **Month goal.** The one-line goal for the month, stated at the top.
   This is the line every candidate is kept or dropped against.
2. **Stakes of failing.** What missing the month goal costs the farmer,
   tied to `docs/farmer-strategy.md` (profit → pressure relief → time
   back). Numbers paired with felt meaning, never bare.
3. **Actionables.** Concrete things the farmer does to move toward the
   goal, grouped by sub-topic (e.g. pruning, climate steering, irrigation,
   feeding/EC, fruit load, variety/rootstock).
4. **Stories** making the other sections more concrete

## Drop rules

* **Off the month goal → drop entirely.** Not relevant to the goal, gone.
* **Not for 80% of the audience → drop entirely.** Audience is market
  gardeners growing indeterminate greenhouse tomatoes. Niche gear or
  practice that most of them won't have (e.g. CO2 injection) is out.
* **Wrong season → drop entirely.** Not actionable in the release month
  or the following 2 months, gone.
* **No expectations of Antoine or Andrew in this file.** Asks to them
  live in the outreach email, not here.


## Format
* No prose
* **Content only, no process talk.** State the goal, stakes,
  actionables, and stories directly. Never describe the method inside
  the file: no "every actionable is kept or dropped against this line",
  no "makes concrete: …" tags, no notes on sourcing or why an entry is
  included. If a line describes our procedure instead of the farming,
  cut it.
* **State what, not why.** Actionables are verb-first instructions.
  Sub-points are 2-4 word fragments, not sentences. No parentheticals
  explaining motivation: delete any clause with "so that", "because",
  or a defensive "without this…". Use `→` to carry flow instead of a
  connecting clause. Trust the reader: they need the instruction, not
  the argument.
