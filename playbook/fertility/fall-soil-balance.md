# Fall soil balance

Organic greenhouse tomatoes, in-ground beds. Run right after
pull-out so amendments react over winter. All inputs
organic-compliant.

Flow: sample → broadfork → leach? → pH → amend.


## Step 1 — Fall soil sample (within 2 weeks of pull-out)

- Before first irrigation of the day
- Wash trowel and plastic bucket first
- 12–15 cores per greenhouse
- Dug knuckle deep, between emitters, skip bed edges
- Mix in and submit ~1lb

**Lab order**:
- Mehlich-3 test
- Buffer pH add-on
- SME test (saturated media extract test) only if no EC meter

Same day: [`scouting/slurry-ec-test.md`](../scouting/slurry-ec-test.md)
per greenhouse. Record. That's the leach read.


## Step 2 — Broadfork

**Every bed, full pass, tines to depth.** Fractures the top
~12 in where greenhouse compaction lives so leach water moves
down, not sideways. Replaces the percolation test.

- Insert every 6–8 in, walking backward; one step, one insertion
- Two passes side by side on a 5 ft bed
- Lean back until the soil lifts and cracks; don't flip
- ~15–20 min per 100 ft bed


## Step 3 — Leach decision

**Read the slurry EC from Step 1** against the salt-buildup
band on the slurry card. No EC meter → SME EC > 4.5 dS/m.

| Read | Do |
|---|---|
| Below salt buildup | Skip to Step 4 |
| Salt buildup | Leach |
| Salt buildup and M3 Na > 50 ppm | Gypsum first, then leach. Ca displaces Na off exchange sites |

- Salty water (softener, brackish well) can't leach. Use rain
  through vents, rain barrel, trucked water.

1. Run the Leach cycle.
2. 2 days after the last pulse: slurry EC again.
3. In band → done. Still salt buildup → another Leach cycle.

One cycle halves salts; that's not always enough.

Reading didn't drop ~40–50% → water isn't moving through.
Fix drainage or drip coverage before more water.

**Leach Cycle** - one size fits all, any soil, any tape
- **Run 4 h per day × 7 days** on a daily timer.
- Aim for the best coverage:
    - Wobbler 10ft appart
    - 4 Drip tapes per bed


## Step 4 — pH correction

Aim **6.2**. Leaves a season of upward drift before 6.5.

**Max 0.5 unit correction per fall**; next fall's test finishes
the job.

Doses per 100 ft × 5 ft bed, per 0.1 unit. Incorporate.
3–6 months to react.

**pH > 6.5 → Tiger 90CR**

| Sand | Loam | Clay |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 lb | 0.9 lb | 1.8 lb |


**pH < 6.0 → pelletized calcitic lime.**

| Sand | Loam | Clay |
|---|---|---|
| 2 lb | 4 lb | 8 lb |


**Flag:** well water drifts pH up +0.1–0.3/year. Annual
maintenance. pH still up two falls running despite sulfur →
test water alkalinity (as CaCO₃). Above 200 ppm → acidify
fertigation to pH 5.5–5.8 with citric acid.


## Step 5 — Fall amendments (immobile nutrients)

| Nutrient | Target (M3) | When low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | 75 ppm | Bone meal |
| K | 190 ppm, ≥ Ca ÷ 8, ≥ Mg ÷ 1.4 | Sulfate of potash |
| Ca | 800 ppm | Gypsum |
| Mg | 125 ppm, ≥ Ca ÷ 11 | Sul-Po-Mag |
| Zn | 4 ppm | Zinc sulfate |
| Mn | 25 ppm | Manganese sulfate |
| Cu | 1.5 ppm | Copper sulfate |
| Fe | 60 ppm | Iron sulfate |
| B | 0.75 ppm | Borax / Solubor, tiny dose; toxicity is 2× sufficiency |

**pH lockup:** dose anyway, same fall as sulfur.

**Don't overdo it:** There are NO proven yield gain associated
with nutrients over target while yield losses DEFINITELY HAPPEN! Also,
undershoot is fixed with routine fertilizer top off while
overshoot on Cu, Mn, B, Mg takes years to fix.

increase needed = target ppm − current ppm
lb/ft² ≈ increase needed × 0.046 ÷ label % ÷ oxide factor ÷ 1000
lb/bed = lb/ft² × bed top width (center to center without alley) × bed length

Label % as a decimal: 50% K₂O → 0.50, not 50.

| Nutrient | Oxide factor |
|---|---|
| P | 0.44 (label is P₂O₅) |
| K | 0.83 (label is K₂O) |
| All others | 1 |


Example: K at 100 ppm, target 190. Sulfate of potash, label
50% K₂O → 0.50, oxide factor 0.83.
lb/ft² = 90 × 0.046 ÷ 0.50 ÷ 0.83 ÷ 1000 = 0.010
Bed top 5 ft × 100 ft = 500 ft² → 5 lb.

**Incorporate knuckle deep**


## What not to do

### No compost

Blind compost brings salts, P overload, K you didn't budget; it
undoes Steps 3 and 5. Skipping it costs nothing for years: OM
drops ~0.1–0.2%/year, a 5% soil is 4% in a decade, fertigation
supplies the nutrients. Worst case is more frequent irrigation
and a yearly broadfork.

Save costs and labor. Skip it.

**OM% under 3% :** Consider compost carefully for structure,
water-holding and the biology that runs the sulfur reaction.
Don't do it blindly or you'll ruin the leach and amendment steps !



### No cover crop, no tarp

Bare soil in a greenhouse: no rain, no erosion, no leaching,
trivial weeds. One winter of cover gains little. Bare and
broadforked is fine.

Save costs and labor. Skip it.
