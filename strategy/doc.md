# The Diagnosis

* The Farm 1.0 requires abusive hours
* How to make a farm profitable on decent hours ?
* Hard to find impactful practice changes when running a diversified farm. Too spread out
* A new farming model that does not depend on working more is necessary to increase how much small farm can accomplish in a week 
* Orisha has new lean business, greenhouse growing and tech skills to develop the Farm 2.0 practices but struggle to get these practice adopted and don't want to operate
* GFM has a good knowledge of American farms, a great audience, a good reputation but struggle to increase it's revenue with magazine subscription
* Orisha has a high value product but can't generate adoption
* GFM has the distribution capacity to generate adoption but lack a revenue potential

---

* We could build that model with the funds
* We need a self-funding way to get there

The crux: There is no market for greenhouse automation
because pro greenhouse growing isn't a practiced, believed-in
category among market gardeners — and the one party with the
distribution to make it a category (GFM) has the audience but
no product worth paying more for. The program is the wedge
that creates the category and becomes the product.


▎ Market gardeners want to live decently without overworking
▎ — a 40-hour week, not more money. The one model that breaks
▎ the hours-for-income tradeoff is professional-yield

▎ controllable, automatable space.

The small-farm world runs on one dominant model — Fortier's
Market Gardener ("Farm 1.0"): start with little capital on
little land. It delivered access, but in practice it demands
punishing labor, so people burn out. No one has defined the
successor: a farm that concentrates high value into a small,
controllable footprint and gives the farmer back a sane work
week (Market Garden 2.0).

The greenhouse, run with automation and lean methods, is the
economic heart of that farm — the one lever that breaks the
hours-for-income tradeoff. Farmers half-believe pro yields are
possible but think it's "not for a farm like mine" and fear it
would eat their week. Automation removes the hours; the
program removes the belief gap. And GFM is the trusted
distribution to make Market Garden 2.0 a movement — giving
Andrew the 21st-century product his saturated magazine needs.
