# Feed cadence — keeping salinity drinkable

Greenhouse tomatoes. Interim fertility guidance: how much
nitrogen, how often, and how to correct off an EC read.

Too much salt is what stops the plant drinking. Feed
**gradually** — hold nitrogen back, let root-zone EC, not
guesswork, tell you when to give more or less.

Interim only. The program covers fertility in more depth
later. Micronutrients (Zn / Mn / Cu / Fe) aren't a focus
here; most growers don't tissue-test.


## The two failure modes

- **Too conservative** → deficiencies.
- **Too generous** → EC climbs → the plant can't drink.

High-yielders fertilize **less** than you'd expect. Salt
build-up is the exception, not the rule.


## When EC read says feed

Run the slurry EC test → `playbook/scouting/slurry-ec-test.md`
for method and per-soil accept bands. An EC pen is ~$80. It
tells you when to give more or less of a **balanced** feed —
not what's missing (that needs a soil test).

| EC read | Do |
|---|---|
| In band | Feed on normal cadence. |
| Low | First rule out leaching, then feed more. |
| High | Wait for it to fall back into band before feeding. |


## Correcting off-band

- **EC low** — check you're not **leaching** with over-long
  irrigation first → then increase fertility.
- **EC high** — hold. Wait for the read to fall into band.
  Give less at a time. You can't front-load your way out.


## Cadence

- Frequent small doses → far easier on the plant than big ones.
- Weighing labor against plant health: **once every 2–3 weeks**
  is a good compromise.
- No front-loading. Big early feeds push the plant leafy /
  vegetative.


## Scaling up with the crop

As the program unlocks more fruit load and growth, the plant
eats more → the feed program must scale up with it.

Organic-fertility lag: microbes take time. There's a delay
between feeding and soil fertility actually changing. Don't
chase it fast.


## Bonus

Staying on-band prevents salt build-up and protects fruit
flavor.
