  find the real cause.

## Body

1. **The strategy is a patch, not a fix** [Guillaume leads]
   - Concept: lower the temperature to slow growth, find the root cause
     (next videos), fix it, then restore the temperature once the stem
     gets bigger than AA.
   - Visible signal: WARNING, vigor correcting does NOT mean the problem
     is fixed. You traded yield to keep the plant productive long term.
   - Number: not more than 5F lower at a time, night and day.

2. **Average is what counts** [Andrew leads, Guillaume reacts]
   - Concept: people stress over spikes, but the average temperature is
     what counts. Spikes stress the plant but aren't enough to tank
     vigor.
   - Visible signal: how to calculate the 24h average, a weighted
     average. Show the bar on the Orisha graph.
   - Number: don't go below 55F to correct the average or a pollen
     problem.

3. **Set the average to load and light** [Guillaume leads]
   - Concept: more fruit load means run cooler; more light allows
     warmer. The target changes daily with cloud cover, daylight,
     season, and dirty plastic.
   - Visible signal: the consultant gives 6 temperatures
     (Sunny/Partly/Cloudy by night/day) as a reference point.
   - Number: encoded in Orisha with number of clusters and a vigor
     slider.

## Conclusion (~20s)

- Restate: REPEAT WARNING, vigor should correct after you lower the
  temperature, but you still need the discipline to fix the root cause
  to get your yields.

## Intro (~30s)

- Hook story: [needed]
- Felt stake: when you can't cool the house, the heat keeps draining the
  plant.
- Topic in one line: vigor is sugar photosynthesized minus sugar
  consumed, and heat hits both sides.

## Body

1. **Heat increases sugar consumption** [Andrew leads]
   - Concept: every cell burns sugar to stay alive, and hot nights burn
     it with no light to refill.
   - Visible signal: hot nights are the quiet killer, zero sugar made
     but burning all night.
   - Number: the burn rate roughly doubles for each 20F rise.

2. **Heat puts pressure through transpiration** [Andrew leads, Guillaume reacts]
   - Concept: if the roots can't supply enough water to transpire, the
     plant stops photosynthesis to preserve water.
   - Visible signal: test leaf temperature.
   - Number: the sunniest summer day exposes the plant to 10x more
     radiation, so 10x more water needs.

3. **What to do on both sides** [Andrew leads]
   - Concept: lower the temperature and control how many cells the plant
     maintains through leaf and fruit pruning; healthy leaves make more
     sugar.
   - Visible signal: preserve water (irrigate to keep transpiration
     going 24/7, keep fertilization from impairing uptake).
   - Number: reduce transpiration flow by stopping HAF and by pruning,
     since more leaves mean more water demand.

## Conclusion (~20s)

- Restate: heat empties the sugar budget from both sides, so defend both
  the making and the spending.
- Action this week: [needed]
- Next video: irrigation.

# Video 6 — Dialing in irrigation by soil type

Status: draft
Target length: 3 min (max 5)
Audience cut: students
Speakers: Antoine

---

## Intro (~30s)

- Hook story: [needed]
- Felt stake: the calculator gives a start, but your soil tells you if
  you're actually right.
- Topic in one line: scoop from the root zone and read the soil by hand,
  the test changes with sand, loam, or clay.

## Body

1. **Where to test (all soils)** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: scoop soil from the root zone.
   - Visible signal: don't trust the surface, dig to your knuckles, as
     often as possible.
   - Number: tweak by 10 to 30% at a time, lean to the dry side.

2. **The hand test by soil** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: sand and loam, squeeze in your fist; clay, push into a flat
     strip.
   - Visible signal: good sand forms a weak crumbly ball with a faint
     damp stain; good loam holds a ball, cool and moist, palm damp but no
     drip; good clay is pliable like putty, takes a thumbprint, ribbons a
     few cm, matte.
   - Number: clay's single best tell is the shine; matte is right, glossy
     means too far.

3. **Don't under-run the drip** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: short pulses waste effort.
   - Visible signal: too wet shows as free water, glisten, muddy palm.
   - Number: it takes about 2 min to fill the drip tapes; irrigating less
     than that is useless.

## Conclusion (~20s)

- Restate: read your own soil at the root zone, by its own test.
- Action this week: dig to your knuckles and run the hand test for your
  soil type before you adjust.
- Next video: prevent growth throttle.

# Video 9 — Fertility keeping the plant from drinking

Status: draft
Target length: 3 min (max 5)
Audience cut: students
Speakers: Antoine

---

## Intro (~30s)

- Hook story: [needed]
- Felt stake: you irrigate perfectly, yet the plant doesn't improve,
  because something blocks it from drinking.
- Topic in one line: too high salinity and the plant can't drink, even in
  soaked soil.

## Body

1. **What too much fertility costs** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: high salinity leads to blossom end rot, deficiencies, and
     vigor issues.
   - Visible signal: you can even see plants wilting in soaked soil.
   - Number: too conservative gives deficiencies, too generous and the
     plant can't drink.

2. **Calibrate with EC** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: simple and cheap; EC won't tell you what's missing (you
     need a soil test) but tells you when to give more or less.
   - Visible signal: measure with a 2:1 ratio (DO IT, SHOW IT): 5 spots
     at root depth, equal weight of distilled water, stir 1 min, stand 1
     min, read mid-liquid in mS/cm. [Cut says 2:1 but also "same weight",
     which is 1:1. Confirm.]
   - Number: target bands, sand 0.5 to 1.0, loam 1.0 to 2.0, clay 1.3 to
     2.7.

3. **Adjust gently** [Antoine leads]
   - Concept: too low, check you aren't leaching with long irrigation,
     then increase fertility; too high, wait for EC to return to band and
     give less at a time.
   - Visible signal: you can't front-load.
   - Number: more frequent doses are easier on the plant; once every 2 to
     3 weeks is a good compromise. Bonus, it prevents salt build-up.

## Conclusion (~20s)

- Restate: keep salinity in band so the plant can actually drink what you
  give it.
- Action this week: run the EC test and compare to your soil's band.
- Next video: useless leaves and how they drain vigor.

     more than the leaves produce.
   - Number: prune flag leaves like normal leaves later.

## Conclusion (~20s)

- Restate: flag leaves rebuild the engine when the plant can't grow full
  leaves.
- Action this week: where leaves are short, pinch suckers into flag
  leaves before the cluster.

## Body

1. **Healthy leaves are your AC** [Guillaume leads]
   - Concept: the earlier tricks grow bigger leaves, which is your
     cooling.
   - Visible signal: bigger leaves raise transpiration 10x and cool the
     canopy by 15F; a healthy bed cools like 2 big window ACs.
   - Number: but if irrigation can't supply the water, the plant stops
     transpiring and you lose all the cooling.

2. **Lower fruit load to buy heat tolerance** [Andrew leads]
   - Concept: fewer fruit leaves more sugar for healthy growth.
   - Visible signal: if you'll pause sales, prune a whole cluster 7 weeks
     before; otherwise lower load per leader evenly.
   - Number: every fruit lowered lets the plant take 2F warmer; prune new
     clusters to 2.5 (alternate 3 and 2), or 2 if vigor doesn't recover.

3. **Use shade last** [Guillaume leads]
   - Concept: shade costs photosynthesis all the time, even when it's not
     sunny.
   - Visible signal: it only helps when roots can't supply water fast
     enough.
   - Number: 1% light is 1% yield; a diffusing 30% shade hopes for 5F
     cooler under the July sun, its biggest win being lower water demand.
