# Month 5: Get ready for next season

Status: draft 2026-09-02 (Stage 1 brainstorm, not yet approved).
Source for the Google Doc Brainstorm tab.
Sources: Turcotte Québec guide (nutrition ch.), Heuvelink ch. 6, Orisha course Step 3 and 5, Gordon and Drew raw notes.

## Month goal

- **Fix in October what the crop could not tell you in July:** nutrient
  lock-out, uneven beds, and broken gear, while the tunnel is empty and the
  season's evidence is still fresh.

## Stakes of failing

- **A lock-out looks like a feeding problem and eats the fix.** Yellow heads,
  pale young leaves, potassium marks despite heavy feed: more fertilizer on a
  blocked root zone costs money and changes nothing.
- **Lock-out grows with yield.** The more pounds you ask, the more the plant
  pulls, the sooner a locked element shows. The tunnel that doubled last year
  is the one that hits it first.
- **The weakest bed sets the schedule.** Prune, irrigate and heat for the
  average → the wet corner rots, the dry end wilts, the crew visits both
  twice.
- **Uneven beds hide in the average.** Weekly pounds look fine while one
  quarter of the plants carry half the load.
- **October is the only month the problem is visible and fixable at once.**
  Where water pooled and where roots drowned is still written in the soil; in
  February it is a memory, in June it is under a crop.
- **A broken pump, a torn roof, a controller still in the box:** each one is
  a June emergency with plants in the way, or an October afternoon.

## Actionables

### Read the season's evidence first

- **Walk each bed with the harvest log:** mark weak stretches, strong
  stretches, the yellow-head rows.
- **Map wet spots before pull-out:** algae on the surface, moss, sclerotinia
  at the stem base, pale leggy plants → same map every year.
- **Map dry spots:** wilting first, soil dry 3 in down, small hard fruit.
- **Mark where air sat still:** condensation, gray mold, leaf mold clusters
  → next year's fan spot.
- **Photograph the deficiency symptoms while leaves are still on:** head
  colour, mid-canopy, old leaves → three photos per bed.
- **Write down what to change while the crop is still up:** variety, feed,
  drip layout, gear.


### Avoid nutrient lock-out

- **Lock-out, not shortage:** the element is in the soil, the root cannot
  take it → adding more does nothing.
- **Higher yield → higher pull → earlier lock-out.** A strong crop on last
  year's program shows the block a heavy-load first.
- **Tell lock-out from shortage:** soil test says enough, plant says
  deficient → lock-out. Both say low → shortage.
- **Match the symptom to the element:**
  - Head pale to yellow, whole leaf, sudden in spring or under heavy load → iron or manganese: pH or root stop.
  - Head yellow between veins, veins green → iron.
  - Old leaves scorched edges, brown flecks, uneven ripening → potassium: antagonism or short supply.
  - Old leaves yellow between veins, bottom up → magnesium: potassium or calcium excess.
  - Blossom end black and sunken, heads fine → calcium transport, not soil calcium.

- **Sudden yellow head after a good run → look at roots first, feed second.**
  Iron and manganese enter at new root tips → anything that stops root
  growth (cold soil, waterlogging, salt, root disease) shows as an iron
  deficiency with iron present.
- **Spring "growth spots" on young leaves are not an iron shortage:** adding
  iron or spraying does nothing → fix the crop conduct (water, root zone,
  temperature).

#### pH lock-out

- **Test pH at pull-out and in spring.** Target 6.0 to 6.5 in soil.
- **Above 6.5 → iron, manganese, zinc, boron, phosphorus lock.** Signs show
  even with large amounts in the soil, especially on loaded plants.
- **Organic tunnels drift up:** repeated compost and generous lime push pH
  toward 6.8 to 7.2 in a few years → test before every lime.
- **Below 5.5 → calcium, magnesium, molybdenum lock.**
- **Bring pH down slowly:** elemental sulfur in fall, ammonium share of
  nitrogen up 1 to 2% at a time. Never in one move.
- **Bring pH up with lime only on a test result**, and count the calcium
  it adds against potassium and magnesium.

#### Cation antagonism

- **Four cations share one door:** potassium, calcium, magnesium,
  ammonium. Too much of one → the others starve.
- **Heavy potassium feed → magnesium deficiency in old leaves.**
- **Heavy calcium (lime, gypsum, hard water) → potassium and magnesium
  drop** → uneven ripening, blotchy fruit.
- **Heavy ammonium → potassium and calcium drop** → blossom-end rot and
  soft fruit.
- **Iron and manganese antagonize each other:** overdosing one creates a
  deficiency of the other.
- **Read the ratios on the soil test, not the single numbers:** base
  saturation K : Mg : Ca in the ballpark of 5 : 15 : 70.
- **Fix the excess before adding the short one:** cut the input that is
  crowding the door, then top up.
- **Gordon's K marks despite 40 lb K/acre:** the potassium was going in;
  something was keeping it out.

#### How to do a soil test

- **Sample right after pull-out:** the season's residue is still in the
  reading, the lab has time before spring.
- **One sample per tunnel, tunnel separate from field.** Covered soil with
  years of fertigation reads nothing like outside.
- **Split a tunnel that behaves unevenly:** weak beds in one bag, strong in
  another.
- **12 to 15 cores, 0 to 6 in, zigzag down the beds**, skip the paths and
  the drip line itself → mix in a clean plastic bucket.
- **Ask for the covered-ground package:** pH, EC / soluble salts, organic
  matter, CEC, base saturation, macros, micros. Salt matters: no rain
  leaches a tunnel.
- **Ask for a saturated-paste or water-extract test if you fertigate:** it
  reads what the roots see this week, not the bank.
- **Same lab, same method, every year.** Numbers only mean something
  against last year's.
- **Test the irrigation water once:** pH, bicarbonates, calcium, iron,
  sodium. Hard water is a hidden lime program.

#### How to do a tissue test

- **In season, not in October:** the tissue test confirms lock-out while
  the crop is up; October is when you plan next year's sampling dates.
- **Sample the most recently mature leaf:** 4th or 5th leaf from the head,
  whole leaf with petiole.
- **20 to 25 leaves per sample, one per plant, same variety, same bed.**
- **Pair a weak bed with a strong bed:** the difference is the answer, not
  the absolute value.
- **Mid-morning, dry leaves, no spray in the last 7 days.** Paper bag, to
  the lab within 24 hours.
- **Book two dates in the calendar now:** first fruit set, and peak load
  late June. Same bed, same leaf, both times.
- **Read tissue against the soil test:** soil high, tissue low → lock-out.
  Both low → feed.

#### Foliar as last resort

- **Foliar is a bandage, not a program:** leaves absorb poorly, bright hot
  spraying burns them → use only while the root zone is being corrected.
- **Works on:** magnesium (Epsom salt), potassium (potassium sulfate),
  nitrogen (urea, 2.5 g/l), iron chelate on the head.
- **Doesn't work on:** calcium for blossom-end rot through the leaf;
  only what lands on the fruit counts. Phosphate sprays burn.
- **Spray at dusk or on a cloudy morning, never in sun.**
- **One spray, then re-test.** If it worked, the block was real → fix the
  root zone this fall.

#### Iron and citric acid

- **Iron locks first above pH 6.5 and in cold wet soil.**
- **Chelate form decides whether it survives:** EDTA fails above pH 6.2 →
  DTPA to 7, EDDHA above that.
- **Chelated iron breaks down in sunlight:** store dark, mix fresh, don't
  leave it in a clear stock tank.
- **Citric acid as the organic acidifier:** drops the stock-solution pH and
  holds iron in solution long enough to reach the roots.
- **Iron sulfate with citric acid** where synthetic chelates are off the
  organic list: dissolve citric first, then the iron, apply through the
  drip, never let it sit.
- **Acidified drip water for pH-locked tunnels:** target 5.8 to 6.2 at the
  emitter, one adjustment at a time, check with strips weekly.
- **Don't chase a yellow head with iron alone:** if roots are stopped by
  water or cold, iron in any form stays outside.


### Uniformity

#### Water and drainage

- **Note where drainage is poor:** puddles 2 h after irrigation, algae,
  moss, sclerotinia at the base, roots brown and short at pull-out.
- **Note where water pools:** low spots along the bed, against the
  baseboard, under a roof drip line, at the far end of a slope.
- **Wet corner → raise the bed or cut a drain**, now, while there is no
  crop.
- **Roof runoff into the tunnel → gutter or a trench along the baseboard**
  before spring melt.
- **Drew's back half stayed wet into May after snowmelt:** last fall's
  drainage work is what dried it.

#### Irrigation evenness

- **Assume the drip is uneven until measured.** Same tape, same bed, the
  far end on a slope gets less.
- **Measure it:** cups under emitters at the start and end of 3 lines →
  run 10 min → lowest cup ÷ average. 90% is excellent, under 80% → fix
  the plumbing.
- **Cut drip tapes shorter:** long runs lose pressure → two short tapes
  fed from the middle beat one long tape fed from the end.
- **Feed the mainline from the uphill end:** gravity works with the
  pressure instead of against it.
- **Sleeve the tape or run it inside a second tape at the wet end** where
  the same line must cross a low spot.
- **Pressure regulator at every bed, 12 psi**, not one at the pump.
- **Pressure-compensating tape** if the tunnel is on a real slope.
- **Same emitter spacing, same tape count on every bed:** 4 tapes, holes
  every 8 in → minutes mean the same thing everywhere.
- **Flush the tapes at pull-out:** open the ends, run clean water until
  it runs clear. Algae and silt are why the far end starved.
- **Filter before the regulator, clean it every month next season.**
- **Replace tape that ran more than two seasons.**

#### Air and heat evenness

- **Consider adding HAF fans:** 1 m/s across the canopy → the still
  corners stop making water.
- **One fan per 50 ft of tunnel, staggered, blowing along the crop**, not
  across the beds.
- **Duct the heater:** poly tube down each walkway, holes every 18 in
  sideways → stops the front row drying out and the far end lagging.
- **Drew's heater-path row ripened first and set less:** the row in the
  hot air ran a different climate all season.
- **Move the climate sensor to canopy height, mid tunnel**, away from the
  heater and the door.
- **Add a second sensor at the weak end** and compare for a week before
  buying anything.


### Maintenance

#### Pump and irrigation

- **Declog the pump:** pull the intake, strainer, impeller → clean, check
  the foot valve.
- **Clean or replace the main filter cartridge or screen.**
- **Flush the mainline, then every tape.**
- **Check every valve and fitting for leaks under pressure.**
- **Drain the pump and lines before frost.**
- **Test the water source:** minerals, pathogens if you can.
- **Calibrate the pH and EC meters:** pH probes last 12 to 18 months →
  replace on schedule, not on failure.
- **Service the fertilizer injector:** seals, suction tube, calibration jug.

#### Structure

- **Fix the roof:** tape or patch every tear, re-tension the plastic, check
  wiggle wire and end-wall seals.
- **Plastic older than 4 years → plan replacement:** light loss is yield
  loss 1 : 1.
- **Wash the plastic inside and out**, 1 gal per 100 sq.ft, soap then
  rinse.
- **Grease and test the roll-up hardware and vent motors.**
- **Service the heater with the tunnel empty:** burner, blower, duct
  joints, exhaust for leaks.
- **Wire the backup thermostat, 3°F below setpoint.**
- **Paint heating pipes and dark surfaces white.**

#### Install the control system

- **Install the controller now, not in spring:** vents, heater, HAF,
  irrigation on one box.
- **Run the wires while there is nothing to trip over.**
- **Set the alerts:** temperature, power, humidity, irrigation fault.
- **Run a full test cycle on a cold October night:** heater relaunch,
  morning vent, dehumidification pulse.
- **Log one week of empty-tunnel climate:** the baseline the spring
  numbers get compared to.
- **Write the spring program now** from this year's log: irrigation
  minutes, setpoints, pulse timing.
