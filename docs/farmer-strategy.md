# Farmer's strategy

The one thing every farmer in the program is working toward, and
the single lever we pull to get there.

## The goal

**Run a profitable market garden in 40 hours/week.**

Profit is the vehicle, not the destination. The real outcome is
pressure relief: shorter days, evenings back, energy for family,
a living from the farm alone. Yield is how we measure the work.
The life change is what the work is for.

## What the yield buys

The beds are already paid for. The hours are already spent. Every
extra vegetable off them is what buys the farmer's time back. Four
ways more yield converts into the life:

- **Fewer beds, same sales** — less walking, weeding, fewer
  problems to chase. The farm gets quieter.
- **Money to hire out what drains you** — pay someone who likes
  doing the accounting to do it. Sundays come back.
- **Means to pay what good people are worth** — better hires who
  stay longer. Less retraining, fewer HR headaches, more energy left.
- **Farm income that carries the household** — the off-farm
  paycheck and the partner's second job become optional again.

## Where to start

**Greenhouse indeterminate tomato yield.**

One bed produces revenue equivalent to ~20-30 beds of beets or
peas. So it is the highest-leverage lever we know how to pull:

- Maximum revenue gain per hour invested.
- Fewer beds to manage: less weeding, irrigation, staff coordination.
- Same farm footprint carries a far lower workload once the tomato
  house pulls its weight.

Everything else in the playbook is downstream of getting that
yield where it can be.
