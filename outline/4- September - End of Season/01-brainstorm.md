## Month goal

- **Turn everything already on the vine into harvested pounds** through
  September and October, and know when the crop stops earning its keep.

## Stakes of failing

- **A third of the season is still ahead.** August to October holds about
  30% of the season's yield potential, roughly $4,500 in a typical tunnel:
  the pounds that flip a break-even season into a profitable one.
- **Losing fruit in the last mile.** Fruit that took a whole season to grow
  can still be lost in the final weeks: gray mold cutting shelf life, cold
  nights stalling ripening, green fruit left hanging at pull-out.
- **Small fruit is a silent 25 to 45% cut.** Same plant count, same hours,
  same trusses, a quarter to nearly half the marketable weight, on a canopy
  that looks healthy the whole time.
- **September to November is a real crop, not a tail.** Québec potential
  over those three months is 1.4 to 2.5 lb/sq.ft: on a 30 by 96 tunnel,
  4,000 to 7,000 lb, a fall paycheque on its own.
- **Carrying the crop into December buys almost nothing:** 0.15 to 0.3
  lb/sq.ft more, against six weeks of the coldest fuel bills of the year.
- **The load can't beat the light.** Extra fruit in fall gives the same
  total weight in smaller fruit → more picking hours for less money.
- **September root loss ends the season early.** Watering mistakes and
  fruit overload on tired roots → dieback → Pythium finishes it.
- **The opposite trap:** heating and staffing a crop past the week it
  stopped paying for itself.
- **A dirty tunnel taxes next season.** Botrytis and leaf-mold spores,
  weeds, and overwintering whitefly all carry over on residue and
  structure.

## Actionables

### ROI: tomatoes against the alternatives

- **Run the fall tunnel decision on numbers, not habit:** weekly pounds ×
  price against hours + fuel.
- **Tomatoes vs winter crops:** the same tunnel in greens against fall
  tomatoes → compare revenue per sq.ft over the same weeks, not per crop.
- **Count the changeover:** pulling early to plant greens costs the standing
  fall tomato crop → put those pounds in the greens column.
- **Tomatoes vs propane:** October light gives barely 0.6 lb/sq.ft against
  1.4 in May → price the fuel per extra pound, not per month.
- **Heating cost is second only to labor:** the fuel line decides whether
  the last weeks pay → know your $/gal and your burn rate before September.
- **December is not worth buying:** 0.15 to 0.3 lb/sq.ft more against the
  coldest six weeks of fuel.
- **Don't spend on extra fruit to buy fall yield:** light sets the ceiling
  → the same pounds arrive as smaller fruit.
- **Set a stop threshold in advance:** the week gross falls below fuel plus
  labor → pull.
- **The same sunlight buys less in fall:** thinner canopy, autumn pounds per
  unit of light run below spring → don't price fall weeks off spring numbers.
- **Every 1% of light lost costs about 1% of the crop:** wash the plastic,
  clear the shade, before the light gets short.

### Heading off (topping)

- **Pick the end date first:** last-harvest week → every decision counts
  back from it.
- **Top 8 to 12 weeks before that week:** cut the growing point → all
  remaining energy goes to the fruit already set.
- **Québec window: September 15 to October 10.** Later topping → longer
  countback.
- **Count back from ripening time, not the calendar:** fall ripening is
  slower than summer → later sets never finish.
- **Don't top late:** fruit size drops, total yield barely moves.
- **Steer generative 2 to 3 weeks before topping:** strong, well set last
  clusters. Weak clusters can't be fixed after topping.
- **Check plant balance the week you top:** too vegetative → slow small
  fruit, heavy sucker regrowth, higher fuel bill. Reproductive → fast
  sizing, fewer suckers.
- **Leave 1 to 2 leaves above the last cluster in fall:** 2 to 4 if
  topping in summer.
- **Top above a cluster that is already set:** above a cluster not yet in
  flower → sizing stalls.
- **Keep suckering after topping:** the plant keeps pushing new heads →
  pinch weekly.
- **Cut flowers and clusters that can't finish:** anything set near the
  topping date won't ripen → remove at flowering.
- **Go back to 5 fruit per cluster 8 weeks out:** heatwave season is over →
  the vigor goes into size, not new growth.
- **Expect very dark green leaves after topping:** normal → keep the shift
  gradual with steady deleafing.
- **Keep watching balance after topping:** too much leaf against the load
  → micro-cracking and uneven ripening.
- **Flower to ripe fruit takes 6 to 9 weeks:** anything flowering after the
  topping week never finishes.
- **Pull the remaining flowers a week after you top.**
- **The countback runs on temperature, not the calendar:** fruit load, light
  and humidity barely move it → a cold fall stretches every number.
- **Leave a few small shoots at the very top:** shade and transpiration on
  the last fruit → less cracking and russeting than a bare head.

### Fall pruning

- **Prune below ripening clusters harder:** light on fruit, airflow up,
  faster ripening.
- **Lower the leaf count as light drops:** September sun can't feed a July
  canopy → fewer leaves per leader than summer.
- **September target: 15 to 17 leaves per plant.**
- **Take leaves from mid canopy, not only from the bottom:** light reaches
  the whole plant instead of the floor.
- **After topping: bottom 2 clusters fully cleared, 2 leaves per cluster up
  top, 8 to 10 mature leaves total.**
- **Walk it down to 6 leaves as you harvest:** each pick → another pass.
- **Spread the remaining leaves over the full plant height:** beats a
  bunch at one level.
- **Deleaf into the head to push generative.**
- **Pull every yellow or fully shaded leaf:** it costs sugar, it doesn't
  make any.
- **Bigger beneficial releases if you change the deleafing pattern:** the
  leaves you remove are where they hatch.
- **Don't strip bare:** fully exposed fruit ripens and rots faster.
- **Deleaf hard at the head, slow at the bottom:** same leaf count, more
  pounds. Stripping bottom leaves early only costs light.
- **Sun on bare fruit runs 18°F above air:** past 86°F no red pigment, past
  104°F the tissue dies white and dry.
- **Leave pulled leaves piled inside a few days if you release parasites:**
  the wasps hatch out before the pile goes to the compost.
- **Keep the Month 3 hygiene:** no stubs, morning pruning, dead material out
  of the tunnel.
- **Cut every clean-out shortcut in now:** clips and strings that come down
  in one pass at pull-out.

### Fall temperature

- **Keep heating into fall:** heat is what keeps the fall pounds coming;
  don't let cold end the season early.
- **Keep nights warm enough:** a too-cold night shuts the plant down →
  ripening stalls for days.
- **Match temperature to the light:** cloudy fall days → run cooler, near
  60°F → warmth without light grows thin, weak plants.
- **Heavy load → cooler days:** ~10 clusters still ripening → aim 70°F.
- **Set the 24 h average by clusters left:** 5 to 6 clusters → never below
  63°F, or ripening drags and quality goes. 3 to 4 clusters → run cooler
  for bigger last fruit.
- **Push the last fruit with warmth:** once topped and the load is dropping
  → raise the average temperature to speed final ripening.
- **Relaunch the heat hard in the morning:** cold night, a little venting,
  heat on → no condensation, plants active by mid morning.
- **Bring the heating setpoint close to the venting setpoint:** aggressive,
  and the only reliable cure for heavy stagnant air.
- **Skip pre-nights if you see cracking:** growth cracks or
  micro-cracking → the technique makes it worse.
- **Move temperature no faster than 2°F per hour:** cold nights make big
  morning swings easy to trigger.
- **Never let it stall:** a crop stopped by cold takes weeks to restart, and
  fall has no weeks to spare.
- **Backup thermostat wired to the heater, 3°F below setpoint:** one frost
  night ends the season.
- **Alerts on temperature and power:** first cold nights are when heaters
  fail.
- **Bank the average, not each night:** 5°F under target for 6 days is fine
  if the next 6 days pay it back → heat when heat is cheap.
- **Nothing grows below 54°F:** that's the floor, not a setback.
- **Cool fall days make bigger fruit:** warmth spreads the same sugar over
  more fruit → running cooler with the load down sizes the last picks.
- **Leaves still curled past 11am → warm the hours between midnight and
  sunrise**, not the afternoon.

### Humidity and disease

- **Fall is condensation season:** cold nights, warm wet inside → keep the
  dehumidification pulses running to the last harvest.
- **Pulse air out 6 min every 40 min while heating:** roll-ups open, heater
  on → dry air in without cold shock.
- **Open roll-ups in stages at sunrise:** fruit warms slower than air →
  condensation lands on the fruit.
- **Watch gray mold on ripening fruit:** it cuts shelf life right when the
  fall harvest lands, days instead of 2 to 3 weeks.
- **Tighten irrigation as days shorten:** last watering no later than 4 h
  before sunset → no wet nights.
- **Log humidity at canopy height overnight:** gut reading fails at night,
  which is when fall humidity does its damage.
- **Keep the air moving:** circulation fans, venting, minimum heat.
  Stagnant air starts the fungal season.
- **Prune only while plants are transpiring:** wait for it in the morning,
  stop early enough that wounds dry before dark.
- **Never prune into a wet, non-transpiring plant.** Root pressure pushes a
  droplet out of every fresh wound → the droplet catches Botrytis spores
  from the air → the plant swallows it back → infection sheltered inside
  the tissue.
- **Cut out stem cankers completely, then paint the wound:** a half cleaned
  canker keeps eating the stem.
- **Strip yellowed truss stubs:** old rafles are Botrytis nurseries.
- **Inspect the deleafing nodes every pass.**
- **Keep the soil surface dry:** fix drip leaks and runoff under the
  gutters, leave no leaves on the ground.
- **Assume Botrytis is already latent:** infected tissue shows nothing
  until conditions turn → act on the first sign, not the first symptom.
- **Whitefly wins the fall:** less light, fewer leaves, cooler tunnel →
  Encarsia and Eretmocerus fall behind → increase releases now, not in
  November.
- **Reinforce the powdery mildew program if it's already in:** fall is not
  the time to ease off.
- **Avoid marginal leaf burn.**
- **Read fruit temperature at sunrise, not air temperature:** fruit warms
  slowest → if the fruit stays dry, the leaves do too.
- **Keep air moving at about 1 m/s:** moving air holds leaf surfaces above
  the dew point. Stagnant air is what makes the water.
- **Pull dropped flower petals off young fruit and out of stem joints:**
  dying petals are how Botrytis gets into the fruit and starts stem cankers.
- **Handle infected tissue gently:** bumping a lesion after a humid night
  releases a visible cloud of spores.
- **Hold humidity under 85% for leaf mold:** it runs at 72 to 75°F, exactly
  the fall house.
- **Running the house cold to save fuel breaks whitefly control:** Encarsia
  and Eretmocerus slow down with the temperature.

### Fruit load and tired roots

- **Stop adding load in early August:** hold it, then bring it down from
  September.
- **Early September target: about 4.5 fruit per sq.ft:** against 5 to 6 in
  the long-day months.
- **Overload steals the last clusters:** too many fruit → weak new
  clusters and smaller fruit across the board.
- **Protect the roots in September:** a summer-worn root system tolerates
  no watering mistakes → dieback, then Pythium.
- **Fall pushes plants vegetative:** cooler nights make vigor easy to
  rebuild → rebuild it, then steer generative again for the last sizing.


### Wind down water and feed

- **Cut volume from October:** fewer plant hours, less demand.
- **Start watering later, stop earlier:** shorter window as days shorten.
- **Longer cycles, fewer of them.**
- **Stop 3 to 4 hours before sunset:** the root zone must dry down
  overnight.
- **Aim for a real overnight dry-down:** roots need air more than water
  once they slow.
- **Feed richer, not drier:** raise concentration, especially potassium →
  never dry-stress the crop for size.
- **Late-season feed runs richest of the season:** potassium and nitrogen
  both step up, EC around 1.8 → the last clusters are the hungriest.
- **A mild squeeze late buys flavour:** moderate water or salt stress in the
  last weeks of fruit fill raises sugars and lycopene with little yield lost.
  Mild, not dry.
- **No swings from dry to soaked:** that pattern makes the cracking and
  ripening defects, not the weather.


### Boost caliber: dehumidify to move calcium

- **Small fruit on a lush plant is a transport problem:** air too wet → no
  transpiration → calcium never reaches the fruit.
- **Hold a real humidity deficit overnight:** below 2 g/m³ costs 25 to 45%
  of marketable weight.
- **Read the deficit, not the RH:** RH at 90% means something different at
  45°F than at 70°F.

| Humidity deficit | Effect | Marketable loss |
|---|---|---|
| 0 g/m³ | air saturated permanently, no xylem flow ever, condensation constant | near total, crop failure |
| 1 g/m³ | trace flow, calcium delivery collapses | 50 to 80% |
| 2 g/m³ | slow chronic flow, plant limps | 25 to 45% |

- **Fix it with air, not inputs:** heat plus open vents in pulses, HAF
  running, tighter evening irrigation.
- **Spray calcium straight onto young fruit** if blossom-end rot is showing:
  the fix is on the fruit, not in the soil.
- **Steer generative if the canopy is winning:** strongly growing leaves
  outpull the fruit for the calcium that is already there.

### Tell it apart from the impostors

- **Guttation at dawn, most mornings, most plants:** root pressure with
  nowhere to push. Strongest tell.
- **Big healthy plant, modest fruit:** the plant spends on leaves because
  the fruit can't take delivery.
- **One week of overnight humidity logging at canopy height settles it.**
- **Not the variety:** "this one just runs small" is the most common wrong
  call.
- **Not soil calcium:** lime or gypsum added, soil test fine, problem
  stays. The calcium was never missing, it couldn't move.
- **Not overwatering, though cutting water helps a little:** drier soil
  forces slightly more air exchange → looks like water was the issue.
- **Not fruit load, though thinning helps a little:** fewer trusses do size
  up → the transport problem stays.
- **The vigorous canopy misleads:** every visual signal says healthy plant.

### Keep going or close

- **Decide with the harvest log, not the gut:** weekly pounds × price vs
  hours + heating → the week the crop stops paying is the week to stop.
- **A weak or diseased crop can still finish:** top now, strip hard, ripen
  what's set, then pull.
- **Write down what to change next season while the crop is still up:**
  variety heat tolerance, cluster pruning dates, humidity gear.
- **Mark every spot where air sat still all season:** that is next year's
  fan location, and you will not remember it in February.


### Bank the last fruit

- **Pick everything at breaker before the last cold night:** first color at
  the blossom end → it finishes indoors.
- **Ripen it warm, dry, and out of the sun:** room temperature, single
  layer, never the fridge.
- **Pick cool, in the morning:** fruit picked hot keeps worse.
- **Don't pick undersized green fruit:** below about 90% of full size it
  turns red and never tastes like anything.
- **Set the finish date with the room temperature.** Days to table ripe:

| Stage at pick | 55°F | 60°F | 64°F | 68°F | 72°F | 77°F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mature green | 18 | 15 | 12 | 10 | 8 | 7 |
| Breaker | 16 | 13 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 5 |
| Turning | 13 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 3 |
| Pink | 10 | 8 | 6 | 4 | 3 | 2 |

- **Never below 54°F:** chilling injury → slow uneven ripening, faster rot,
  pitted skin, flat taste.
- **Never above 86°F either:** the red pigment stops and the fruit goes
  orange.
- **Cold nights on the plant cost shelf life after the pick:** fruit chilled
  on the vine carries the damage into the crate.
- **Ripe fruit keeps 3 to 5 days, green fruit keeps 2 weeks:** sort the last
  picks by that, not by looks.
- **Give the ripening crates airflow:** ripening fruit makes its own heat.
- **Sort the last picks by finish date:** a breaker and a half-ripe don't
  go in the same crate for the same market.
- **Taste-calibrate the last harvests:** fall fruit colors up before it
  tastes ripe → pick one, eat it, set the standard.
- **Tell customers the season is ending two weeks out:** last-tunnel-tomato
  weeks sell out.
- **Sell the seconds deliberately:** cracked and small fruit → sauce,
  bulk boxes, restaurant seconds, not the compost.


### Wind down the labour

- **Drop maintenance passes as the load drops:** topped plants need
  suckering and deleafing, not full weekly care.
- **Cut the tasks that only pay on a growing plant:** clipping, lowering,
  training all stop at topping.
- **Keep the ones that protect the fruit still hanging:** deleafing for
  air, disease scouting, harvest.
- **Move the freed hours into fall crops and the close-out**, or take them
  back.
- **Name the week the crew's tunnel hours end:** the pull-out date sets it.


### Pull the crop

- **Cut plants at the base and let them dry standing for a few days:** dry
  vines come out lighter and cleaner.
- **Strip clips and strings in one pass down the row.**
- **Get every scrap of vine and leaf out of the tunnel:** residue left
  inside is next season's Botrytis and leaf mold.
- **Compost the vines well away from the tunnels**, or off site if the crop
  was diseased.
- **Pull the roots where you can:** disease and nematodes overwinter in
  them.
- **Leave the tunnel empty and open a few days before washing.**


### Reset the soil

- **Sample right after pull-out:** the tunnel's own season is still in the
  reading, and the lab has time before spring.
- **Sample the tunnel separately from the field:** covered ground with
  years of fertigation reads nothing like outside.
- **Watch salt build-up under cover:** no rain leaching → test for it.
- **Plant a cover crop the same week you pull**, or as soon after as the
  calendar allows.
- **Pick the cover crop by exit date:** something that winter-kills if you
  plant early in the tunnel, something that overwinters if you don't.
- **Water the cover crop in:** covered soil gets no rain.
- **Add slow amendments now, not in spring:** compost and lime need the
  months.
- **Break the tomato-after-tomato pattern where the plan allows.**


### Close out the tunnel

- **Wash the whole tunnel, inside and out:** roof, structure, ground.
  Soapy water, sprayer or pressure washer.
- **Use far more water than feels necessary:** 1 gallon per 100 sq.ft.
- **Rinse after soap:** soap film kills disinfectant.
- **Destroy every weed:** green tissue is where whitefly and spores
  overwinter.
- **Clean and disinfect harvest bins, pruners, knives.**
- **Put the yellow sticky traps back up:** blue too if thrips were a
  problem.
- **Add the fans you marked during the season.**
- **Service the heater with the tunnel empty:** burner setting, even heat
  distribution, leaks at the joints.
- **Check and adjust the vents and their motors:** grease the hardware.
- **Paint heating pipes and dark surfaces white:** free light next spring.
- **Flush and check the irrigation system:** filters, valves, leaks.
- **Test the water source:** minerals, and pathogens if you can.
- **Calibrate the sensors and meters:** pH probes last 12 to 18 months.
- **Fix every defect you logged during the crop:** no plants in the way is
  the whole point of this window.

## Stories

- **Gordon, early September, the fold-or-fight moment.** "We may be close
  to the point where I don't think it's worth it to continue this level of
  maintenance, and we'll just let the plants go and try to harvest last
  ripe fruit in early September."
- **Gordon's retrospective lesson.** Prune fruit earlier to preserve plant
  vigor for fall productivity; pick heat-resistant varieties from the
  start.
- **Drew, August 30.** "As of today, we have DOUBLED tomato production
  compared to last year," with weeks of harvest still ahead: the
  end-of-season weeks are where a doubled season total gets banked.
