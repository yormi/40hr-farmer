### --- Sandy soil - Dialing-in irrigation

- Where to test
    - Scoop soil from the root zone
    - Don't trust the surface. Dig to your knuckles. As often as possible

- Analyse
    - Squeeze soil in your fist
    - Good
        - Darkens when you squeeze
        - Forms a weak, crumbly ball that holds for a moment, then falls apart when you bounce it on your hand
        - Leaves a faint damp stain on your palm
    - Too dry
        - Pours through your fingers like dry beach sand
        - Stays pale
        - Leaves your palm clean and dry
        - No shape at all.
    - Too wet
        - Squeezes into a slick blob
        - Free water beads on the surface
        - Your palm comes away muddy

- How to adapt to results
    - We recommend to tweak with 10 to 30% at a time
    - Lean on the dryer side. We tend to keep it too humid
    - Don't forget that it usually takes 2min to fill the drip tapes. Irrigating less than that is useless

### --- Clay soil - Dialing-in irrigation

- Where to test
    - Scoop soil from the root zone
    - Don't trust the surface. Dig to your knuckles. As often as possible

- Analyze
    - Push soil up between thumb and forefinger into a flat strip
    - Good
        - Pliable like fresh modeling clay / putty
        - Takes a thumbprint and holds the print cleanly
        - Ribbons out a few cm before it breaks
        - Surface is matte, not shiny
        - Stains your fingers but isn't muddy.
    - Too dry
        - Hard clods
        - Won't take a thumbprint
        - The ribbon cracks apart right away or won't form
        - Breaks into hard chunks.
    - Too wet
        - Surface goes glossy and slick, like wet pottery clay
        - Smears and sticks to your hand
        - Ribbon sags and flops
        - Squeeze and water seeps.
    - The single best tell:
        - The shine
        - Properly moist clay is matte
        - The moment it looks wet and glossy, you've gone too far

- How to adapt to results
    - We recommend to tweak with 10 to 30% at a time
    - Lean on the dryer side. We tend to keep it too humid
    - Don't forget to count the time it takes to fill the drip tapes. Irrigating less than that is useless

### --- Fertility keeping the plant from drinking

- We spent many videos on irrigation. Maybe you got frustrated. You seem to irrigate perfectly, yet your plant is not improving.
- There's actually something we do that can block the plant from drinking that water.
- Too high salinity and the plants struggle to drink leading to:
    - Blossom end rot
    - Deficiencies
    - Vigor issues
    - You can even see plants wilting in a soaked soil

- Like with irrigation, it's hard to find the right fertility recipe
    - Fruit load, irrigation, greenhouse temperature, soil pH, what's in the soil, etc. all impact fertility
    - Be too conservative and you get deficiencies
    - Be too generous and your plant can't drink
    - Organic fertility is tricky. The time microbe takes to do their job means a lag between our action and the soil fertility actually changing
    - But the new thing with the program: you unlock fruit load and growth, you plant is going to eat more and your program will need to adjust.

- How to know if your okay
    - To keep things simple, and cheap, we calibrate using Electrical Conductivity, EC
    - Won't tell you what's missing, you need a soil test for that, but it'll tell you when to give more or less of a balanced recipe. 
    - Measure EC with a 2:1 ratio (DO IT, SHOW IT)
        1. Get dirt from 5 spots at root-zone depth (~6 in) → combine in its cup, skip obvious stones / roots.
        2. Scoop soil into the cup → note its weight.
        3. Add the same weight of distilled water.
        4. Stir 1 min.
        5. Let it stands 1 min.
        6. Put the sensor in the mid-liquid zone. Not in the sludge at the bottom.
        7. Read the value in mS/cm
    - You want it between :
        - Sand: 0.5 – 1.0
        - Loam: 1.0 – 2.0
        - Clay: 1.3 – 2.7

- How to adjust
    - Too low
        - Make sure you are not leaching nutrients with long irrigation
        - Increase fertility
    - Too high
        - Wait for the EC reading to get back in the target band for your soil
        - Give less at a time.
            - You can't just front-load
            - It's way easier on the plant more frequent doses are 
            - Considering labor time, we see that once every 2-3 weeks is a good compromise

- Bonus: It'll help you prevent salt build-up. Yay !

### --- Small leaves are a weak engine

- When your plants are weak, they grow smaller leaves.

    - Greenhouse temperature
    - All 3 influence vigor and make your life harder

- When to use flag leaves ?
    - Everytime you
        - Get small leaves (happens often with vigor issues)
        - Pruned damaged leaves below the ideal count

### --- Last resort when you can't control the temperature

- We covered what impacts the vigor in the last video. Even if you temperature is not optimal, your plant can tolerate way more than we think if we follow these good practice diligently.

- Before applying the tips in this video, make sure you've followed all the recommendations in the other videos.
    - One side effect of the tricks above is to help plants grow bigger leaves. That increases the amount of transpiration by 10x and cool the canopy of 15'F.
    - Every bed with healthy leaves with enough water to transpire cools the canopy like of 2 big window AC.
    - If the irrigation does not supply enough water though, the plants stop transpiring and you lose all that cooling effect

- For warmer region where the climate is more intense, here's what I suggest as a last resort to keep plant productive through the fall:
    - Fruits consume a lot of sugar. The more fruits a plant bear, the less sugar is left for healthy growth.
        - If you know you pause sales or have a period of lower demand, prune a whole cluster 7 weeks before that moment
        - Otherwise, lower the fruit load per leader evenly
        - Every fruit you lower allow the plant to support 2'F warmer before vigor drops
        - Prune new clusters to
            - 2.5 fruits/cluster (alternate one cluster of 3, one of 2 -> Consistency)
            - 2 fruits/cluster if vigor does not recover
        - if you need a quick fix, you can do it on older clusters that hasn't reached full-size fruits
    - Use shade last:
        - 1% light = 1% yield
        - Shades is extra complexity + limits the photosynthesis 100% of the time.
        - Since the shade is there even when it's not sunny... that means you sacrifice photosynthesis... so less sugar for the plant for the same fruit load -> less vigor
        - Shade only helps when the root system cannot supply water fast enough for the transpiration need
        - If you do use a diffusing 30% shade
            - Theory says you can hope too keep 5'F cooler under the July sun
            - Biggest win is to reduce the water demand of the plant

    - The night is when it's the most important to keep temperature down
    - Careful with the humidity

### Can't cool the greenhouse anymore?

- Vigor = sugar photosynthesized minus sugar consumed

- Heat increase sugar consumption
    - Every cell burns sugar to stay alive. That rate roughly doubles for each 20°F rise.
    - Hot days, and especially hot nights, make the plant burn through the day's sugar faster.
    - Hot nights are the quiet killer: no light, so zero sugar photosynthesized, but the plant is burning sugar all night.
    - What to do:
        - Lower temperature
        - Control how many cells the plant has to maintain = Leaf and fruit pruning
        * Healthy leaves = more photosynthesis

- Heat puts pressure on your plant.
    - The sunniest summer day expose the plant to 10x more radiation -> 10x more water needs.
    - If the root can't supply enough water to transpire, the plants stops photosynthesis to preserve water
    - Leaf temperature to test
    - What to do:
        - Preserve water availability
            - Irrigate to to keep transpiration going 24h/7 without degrading root uptake capabilities
            - Keep fertilization from imparing water uptake
        - Reduce transpiration water flow
            - Stop HAF -> Lower humidity shock
            - Pruning -> More leaves = more water demand
