# Market Garden 2.0 — Partnership Strategy (Orisha × GFM)

Short business plan for the 40hr Farmer program as a joint Orisha × GFM
venture. Built on Rumelt's strategy kernel: diagnosis → guiding policy →
coherent actions. Horizon: 2 years. OK to start from where we are today;
this defines direction.

Status: in progress. Diagnosis locked 2026-06-05. Guiding policy + actions
+ pricing/split/responsibilities still open.

---

## Glossary (strategy terms)

- **Market Garden 2.0** — the banner/category this strategy defines and
  aims to own. The successor to Fortier's *Market Gardener* model ("Farm
  1.0"): a small farm that concentrates high value into a small,
  controllable footprint and returns a sane work week (the 40-hour week),
  instead of low-capital-but-punishing-labor. Greenhouse is its economic
  heart; lean methods + a sane work week are riders.
- **Greenhouse profit engine** — the concentrated pivot point of the
  strategy: professional-yield greenhouse growing run with Orisha
  automation. The one lever that breaks the hours-for-income tradeoff.
- **Farm 1.0** — shorthand for the dominant prior model (Fortier's
  *Market Gardener*): little capital, little land, but punishing labor →
  burnout. The thing Market Garden 2.0 succeeds.

## 1. Diagnosis — the crux (LOCKED 2026-06-05, revised same day)

The small-farm world runs on one dominant model — Fortier's *Market
Gardener* ("Farm 1.0"): little capital, little land. It delivered access,
but it requires abusive hours, so people burn out. No one has defined the
successor: a farm that concentrates high value into a small, controllable
footprint and returns a sane work week (Market Garden 2.0). Farmers don't
know how to farm on decent hours, and a diversified operation is too spread
out to find a single high-impact change on their own.

An **emerging, continuously-improving** path off Farm 1.0 exists —
concentrate a farm's value into a lean, automated greenhouse so a decent
living fits in a decent week. It is somewhat proven and still improving; we
present it as a **common journey**, not a finished system.

It is under-adopted **not for lack of capital.** Farmers already spend
money; they just spend it in the wrong place. The structure is already
owned (we target existing greenhouses), and Orisha's automation is a few
hundred dollars a month with **no upfront cost**. The real gap is **demand
generation**: nobody is showing growers like them that it is real, for
them, and within reach.

The two parties who could generate that demand each hold only half — that
split is the crux:

- **Orisha** has the method, the greenhouse + tech + lean skill, a
  high-value product, and proof-in-progress — but no audience, no demand
  engine, and no wish to operate farms.
- **GFM** has a sizable, trusted audience (~5,000 subscribers, roughly a
  third a fit for the program + Orisha; ~50,000 Instagram; a podcast of
  unknown reach) and deep knowledge of American farms — but a stalled
  subscription-revenue model. **Healthy reach, broken monetization.**

Each company is the missing half of the other. **The program fuses them:
Orisha's method + automation carried by GFM's reach, concentrated on
retrofitting existing greenhouses, presented as a shared journey to Market
Garden 2.0.** Orisha's *proximate* crux is **demand for automation**;
Market Garden 2.0 is the vehicle for it, not a second mission to fund.

### Supporting structure

- **Farmer's real goal:** live decently without overworking (the 40-hour
  week), not "make more money." Income is a constraint, not the desire.
  Connects to mental-health / burnout.
- **The engine raises value per hour, not output per week.** The farmer
  spends the surplus by choice — as time (work less) or income (earn more).
  Brand leads with time. This avoids drifting back into productivity-
  maximizing / overwork.
- **Concentration of force, at the farm level.** A diversified market
  garden spreads effort across dozens of crops; no single tweak moves the
  needle. The greenhouse is the one place to concentrate — the farm-level
  proof that it is the correct pivot.
- **Binding belief (primary):** self-relevance — "real, but that's for big
  industrial ops, not a farm like mine." Plus a know-how gap: even
  believers don't know how to run it on decent hours. Close with: "and it
  won't cost me my week" (automation + the program's method).
- **Why automation is not just an upsell:** pro yields normally cost hours
  (climate + irrigation babysitting). The 40-hour goal forbids adding
  hours. Automation is the only thing that makes pro yields and a sane week
  compatible.
- **Constraint — Orisha does not operate.** Orisha enables (method +
  product + tech); it does not run farms. Rules out franchise / managed-
  farm / run-the-operation business models downstream.

### Scope boundary (from diagnosis)

- **Target existing greenhouses (retrofit), not build-from-scratch.**
  Structure is already owned → no capex barrier; "they already have
  tunnels" supports it. Build-from-scratch is a later, harder segment.
- **Capital is not the binding constraint.** Farmers already spend; they
  misallocate. Automation is opex (~few hundred $/mo, no upfront). The
  demand-gen job is *redirecting existing spend* toward the high-leverage
  lever, not raising capital.
- **Geography bounds the serviceable market.** The live objection "wrong
  climate" = outdoor heat too high to cool the greenhouse enough. Real
  physical ceiling, not a belief. Strongest fit: temperate/cold climates
  (Quebec, northern US, Canada) where the greenhouse retains heat and
  extends season. Hot regions weaken the thesis.

### Value ladder (Brunson), draft 2026-06-10

Narrative spine: the **virtual consultant** gains capability rung by rung
— first knowledge, then **eyes** (sensor), then **arms** (actuation).

| Rung | Offer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | video content on current monthly goal + community forum | $0 |
| **Program** | virtual consultant app (guides farmer alongside content), spreadsheets, coaching calls, FAQ videos, past-goal video library | ~$18/mo |
| **Eyes** | greenhouse sensor feeding the virtual consultant | ~$20/greenhouse/mo — business case to validate |
| **Arms (entry)** | Helper $130/mo, or irrigation control $40/mo | |
| **Arms (full)** | Chief Grower | $230/mo |
| **Year 2+** | incubator / accelerator (build-your-Farm-2.0, high touch) | TBD |

**GFM economics (locked 2026-06-10):** rev-share on the program tier
**plus** a trailing rev-share on every rung (sensor, Helper, irrigation,
Chief Grower) for farmers GFM sources. Pays Andrew to sell the whole
journey, not just the cheap rung. Percentages TBD.

**Coaching calls:** 1 hr/week, group format. Fixed cost regardless of
member count → scales at $18/mo.

Open issues: rev-share percentages; virtual-consultant app build effort vs
gamble-shrinking goal (v1 = human-curated guide + spreadsheets?); sensor
business case; supersedes products.md pricing ($40/mo standalone, free w/
