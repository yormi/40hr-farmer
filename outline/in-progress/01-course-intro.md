# Video outline, course intro — What if we didn't have to run so much

Status: draft
Target length: 3 min (max 5)
Audience cut: students (course members, opening video)

---

## Intro (~45s)

- Hook story (Guillaume, first person, condensed from email 2.1 John composite): "I've spent almost 10 years listening to market gardeners, and I keep hearing the same story. Farmer starts with a vision: steward the land, feed the community, build a life. Then management creeps in. Financial pressure builds. So they double the hours: more beds, more products, more markets, maybe a transformation kitchen. They hit a wall working 12 hours a day, 7 days a week, and the money still isn't there. Exhaustion shows up. The fun is gone. And the worst part: if working harder doesn't help, what does?"
- Felt stake: it's not just the hours. It's the years gone, the crew worn out, the vision blurred. Working more stopped moving anything.
- Topic in one line: this course is about the farmers who got out of that loop, and what they actually changed.

## Body (3 points)

1. **The shift, from firefighting to overseeing**
   - Concept: the farms that got out stopped reacting all day and started running the farm like a predictable machine they oversee. Work doesn't disappear; it becomes plan-able.
   - Visible signal: harvest days hit the projection. Irrigation runs without anyone checking. The crew knows what next week looks like by Sunday night.
   - Number: Antoine (named farmer, on the program) cut his team in half and grew sales 15% in one year. He freed enough time that he now drives 2 hours each way, 3 days a week, to coach others. *(Pending Antoine consent + number verification per memory.)*

2. **What the firefighting actually costs (stories we've lived)**
   - Three scenes paired with their felt cost. Said straight, no scaffolding:
     - **Family holidays you don't go to**, because the farm doesn't allow a real day off. The cost isn't a missed dinner. It's the slow distance you didn't mean to put between you and the people you love.
     - **The crew brings a problem to the table, and you're too tired to engage with it.** You put it down. They feel let down. Next time, they bring less. The farm loses the one thing that compounds: people thinking with you.
     - **You catch yourself impatient with people moving at a normal pace.** Off-farm conversations feel like friction. You're running so hard at work that you can't slow down anywhere else, and the people you care about are paying the price for the farm's pace.
   - Felt cost summary: the farm isn't just spending your hours. It's spending your patience, your relationships, and your team's investment. A farm that eats those things eats its own future.

3. **What "better" actually looks like**
   - Concept: a farm built so the operator chooses what to engage with on a given day, instead of reacting to what's on fire.
   - Visible signal: the phone stops ringing about emergencies. Real decisions land in weekly review, not in panic on a Tuesday afternoon.
   - Number: one greenhouse bed bringing what 20 field beds bring (per [`landing/index.html` leverage section](../../landing/index.html)). That's the income lever that buys the time and team room for the rest of the change.

## Conclusion (~30s)

- Restate: the gap isn't between hard work and easy work. It's between reacting to the farm and overseeing it.
- Action this week: pick one moment from last season the farm took from you that you'd want back. A holiday missed. A conversation cut short. Someone you snapped at. Write it down in one line. The course is about getting it back.
- Next video: the lever we start with, greenhouse tomatoes, and why one bed pays for the rest of the change.
