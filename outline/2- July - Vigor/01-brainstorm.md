# Month 2: Take Control of Plant Vigor

Status: locked 2026-06-04 (Stage 1 brainstorm seed). Source for the
Google Doc Brainstorm tab.

## Month goal

- **Steer plant vigor on purpose through the summer**, so the tomato bed
  hits its yield potential instead of running too leafy or stalling under
  heat.


## What vigor is
- **Vigor** = Health indicator. Stem diameter. Thin = problem to come. Thick =
  waste.

## Stakes of failing

Failing the goal means lost yield off the one bed that earns what 20 to
30 beds of beets or peas do. Lost yield is time not bought back: the beds
you hoped to drop stay, the Sundays stay spent, the off-farm paycheck
stays necessary. Vigor going wrong drains yield four ways.

- **Fewer clusters harvested all season.** Stalled or too-leafy growth
  slows the plant's pace, so it sets and finishes fewer clusters before
  the season ends. A bed can do over 1.4 lb/sq.ft in the best weeks
  versus barely 0.6 late, so every cluster lost in summer is the most
  valuable one.
- **Slower ripening.** Fruit buried in a dense canopy or held back by the
  wrong climate ripens up to 4 weeks late, pushing harvest out of the
  high-price window into the low.
- **Flower and fruit abortion.** Heat, stress, or cloudy stretches make
  clusters drop their blossoms or abort set fruit, erasing whole clusters
  before they ever reach harvest.
- **Disease losses.** A dense, humid canopy and stressed plants feed
  disease (bacterial spot and the like), wiping out fruit and the gains
  already made.

## Actionables

### Reading vigor (no tools, the skill the rest rests on)

- **Stem one foot below the head:** AA-battery thick = balanced; spindly =
  spending more than it makes; fat/oversized = too vegetative.
- **Highest open flower cluster:** 5 to 7 in from the top = balanced; at the
  tip = generative or too dry; over 7 in down = too vegetative or too wet.
- **Vegetative vs generative balance** is one of the levers to keep vigor
  right, not vigor itself. The other levers: climate, irrigation, pruning,
  feeding, fruit load.
- **Rule of thumb:** stress pushes generative; comfort pushes vegetative.
- **The look:** vegetative = long dark leaves, trusses straight up;
  generative = thinner head, short arching trusses, flowers near the tip.

### Climate steering

- **Go into the hot season with maximum vigor:** Otherwise, that's a sign your
  plant is already exhausted and it'll only get worse when heat comes.
- **Temperature average** Instant temperature = no big deal. Important = average. How to calculate.
  Sensor in the wrong place overstates temperature. Show where to install it.
- **Day vs night temperature:** flat profile = vegetative; wide day-to-night gap = fruit.
- **Night floor:** lower nights offset hot days, only to a point (~60F floor).
- **Temperature vs fruit load:** more clusters = run cooler. Show how Orisha does it.
- **Cloudy days:** drop toward 63F; warmth without light grows weak stems.

### Irrigation

- **Touch test:** in sun a working leaf feels cooler than air; if not, the
  plant has stopped transpiring and growth has stalled.
- **Pattern:** many small even waterings = energy to leaf; fewer, larger, longer
  overnight drydown = energy to fruit.
- **Follow foliar area:** More foliar area = more water (consistent pruning is important,
  leaf length as well)
- **Too much water:** suffocating root + root disease
- **Too little water:** Photosynthesis stops -> Less energy to sustain plant -> Vigor drop
- **Heat wave:** +30% water on a 95F day, revert when it breaks (else split
  fruit, feed disease).
- **Single vs 2 leaders:** 2 leaders require stronger roots
- **Last irrigation:** hours before sunset.
- **Over-irrigation tell:** growth difference between middle-of-bed and end-of-bed plants.

### Pruning

- **Leaf length:** Should exceed the elbow. If half of that, need 3x more
  leaves. Problem ferme Decembre.
- **Weekly, ~18 leaves per leader:** more leaves = energy to leaves; harder prune =
  energy to fruit, and settles a crop that started too vegetative.
- **Generative trick:** pull the leaf opposite a young flower cluster.
- **Suckers:** snap on time by hand; a late sucker grows as big as the main
  stem and steals energy. Once a week. Don't skip it!
- **Flag leaves:** keep them when the plant runs short on leaves.
- **Too many leaves can cause wilting.** Antoine's cucumbers.
- **Cut without stubs:** blade or clean snap.

### Fruit load

- **Fruit per cluster:** thin to 3 fruit/cluster 8 weeks before heat-wave
  season; too much fruit exhausts the plant.
- **Kept too much fruit into July:** recovery moves.

### Feeding

- **Hold nitrogen back:** no front-loaded fertilizer (pushes too leafy);
  feed gradually.
- **Keep EC in check:** too high EC and the plant cannot drink. Drought
  symptoms appear... even in a soaked soil.
- **Right nutrients available for the plant** Story at Ferme Decembre (PH)

### Plant
- **Grafts are strong:** 15% more vigorous. They need to be beaten harder.
- **Varieties:** Some are skinnier and it's okay: Pink ID for instance. Cherry
  tomatoes.
