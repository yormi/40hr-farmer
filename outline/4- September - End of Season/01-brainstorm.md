# Month 4: Get the most of the end of the season

Status: draft 2026-07-13 (Stage 1 brainstorm seed, not yet approved).
Source for the Google Doc Brainstorm tab.

## Month goal

- **Turn everything already on the vine into harvested pounds** through
  September and October, and know when the crop stops earning its keep.

## Stakes of failing

- **A third of the season is still ahead.** August to October holds about
  30% of the season's yield potential, roughly $4,500 in a typical tunnel:
  the pounds that flip a break-even season into a profitable one.
- **Losing fruit in the last mile.** Fruit that took a whole season to grow
  can still be lost in the final weeks: gray mold cutting shelf life, cold
  nights stalling ripening, green fruit left hanging at pull-out.
- **The opposite trap:** heating and staffing a crop past the week it
  stopped paying for itself.

## Actionables

### Heading off (topping)

- **Pick the end date first:** last-harvest week → every decision counts
  back from it.
- **Top the plants ~6-8 weeks before pull-out?:** cut the growing point →
  all remaining energy goes to the fruit already set.
- **Leave 2 leaves above the top cluster:** feed and shade the last fruit.
- **Keep suckering after topping:** the plant keeps pushing new heads →
  pinch weekly.
- **Cut flowers and clusters that can't finish:** anything set near the
  topping date won't ripen → remove at flowering.

### Fall pruning

- **Prune below ripening clusters harder:** light on fruit, airflow up,
  faster ripening.
- **Lower the leaf count as light drops?:** September sun can't feed a
  July canopy → fewer leaves per plant than summer.
- **Keep the Month 3 hygiene:** no stubs, morning pruning, dead material
  out of the tunnel.

### Fall temperature

- **Keep heating into fall:** October light gives barely 0.6 lb/sq ft vs
  1.4 in May → heat is what keeps the fall pounds coming; don't let cold
  end the season early.
- **Keep nights warm enough:** a too-cold night shuts the plant down →
  ripening stalls for days.
- **Match temperature to the light:** cloudy fall days → run cooler, near
  60°F → warmth without light grows thin, weak plants.
- **Heavy load → cooler days:** ~10 clusters still ripening → aim 70°F.
- **Push the last fruit with warmth:** once topped and the load is
  dropping → raise the average temperature to speed final ripening.

### Humidity and disease

- **Fall is condensation season:** cold nights, warm wet inside → keep the
  dehumidification pulses running to the last harvest.
- **Watch gray mold on ripening fruit:** it cuts shelf life right when the
  fall harvest lands.
- **Tighten irrigation as days shorten:** last watering no later than 4 h
  before sunset → no wet nights.

### Keep going or close

- **Decide with the harvest log, not the gut:** weekly pounds × price vs
  hours + heating → the week the crop stops paying is the week to stop.
- **A weak or diseased crop can still finish:** top now, strip hard, ripen
  what's set, then pull.
- **Harvest breaker-stage fruit before closing:** fruit showing first
  color ripens off the vine → no pounds left hanging at pull-out.

## Stories

- **Gordon, early September, the fold-or-fight moment.** "We may be close
  to the point where I don't think it's worth it to continue this level of
  maintenance, and we'll just let the plants go and try to harvest last
  ripe fruit in early September."
- **Gordon's retrospective lesson.** Prune fruit earlier to preserve plant
  vigor for fall productivity; pick heat-resistant varieties from the
  start.
- **Drew, August 30.** "As of today, we have DOUBLED tomato production
  compared to last year," with weeks of harvest still ahead: the
  end-of-season weeks are where a doubled season total gets banked.
