# Video outline, Take advantage of the fall opportunity

## Intro (~40s)

- Hook story: Drew, August 30. "As of today, we have DOUBLED tomato production compared to last year," with weeks of harvest still ahead. The doubled season total wasn't banked yet. The fall weeks are where it got banked.
- Felt stake: you just went through August and you want the coming years to be easier. Successful farmers have one thing in common, they found ways to make more money for the same hours. That's the idea behind the 40hr Farmer program.
- Topic in one line: for a lot of farms, fall greenhouse tomatoes are one of the greatest opportunities, and done right, one of the strategic changes that makes more money for the work we put in. This month's goal: take advantage of it.

## Body (3 points)

1. **The break-even clock: fall is where the profit lives**
   - Concept: you spend labor and money on this crop four months before you pick a ripe tomato. Two months of propagation and grafting, two more from first flower to first ripe fruit. A chunk of the season only pays that back. Once past break even, every extra week of strong harvest is pure profit, so keeping harvest going as strong and as long as possible is where the money is.
   - Visible signal: the harvest crates in September look the same as July's, but these are the ones you keep.
   - Number: growers in the program have already harvested 4.5 lb/ft² and still have 3.2 lb/ft² ahead, 7.7 total, about $17k per bed. Felt translation: the fall pounds alone are double what an average grower harvests in a whole season.

2. **Fall is a real crop, not a tail**
   - Concept: September to November carries real yield if the plant is set up for it. Around 4 fruits per cluster, 6 to 8 clusters still to come after top off.
   - Visible signal: trusses still setting and sizing above the last harvest point.
   - Number: Québec potential is 1.4 to 2.5 lb/ft² over those three months, 4,000 to 7,000 lb in a 30 by 96 tunnel. August to October holds about 30% of the season's yield potential, roughly $4,500 in a typical tunnel, the pounds that flip a break-even season into a profitable one.

3. **It's not too late, wherever you are**
   - Concept: two seasons are still ahead of you. Set 4 fruits per cluster as soon as you can control temperature again (follow the weekly check-in recommendations). If the season has been rough, diseased or behind, this is still salvageable: reduce leaf count and open airflow, leaves matter less once the last cluster is done sizing. Worst case you prove the concept for next year and go in confident.
   - Visible signal: Québec growers picking into November while the average farm pulled out in September.
   - Number: Québec growers who continue to November land 4 lb/ft² against a 1.6 lb/ft² average. At Décembre in Quebec City, where January nights average 0°F, we transplanted in December last year and added a second bay this August to supply our customer year round. Margins are thin but we're confident. The further south you are, the more light and temperature work in your favor: one grower on the coaching calls grows through the Florida winter and skips summer entirely.

## Conclusion (~30s)

- Restate: a third of the season is still ahead, and it's the profitable third.
- Action this week: set your plant up for the fall crop, 4 fruits per cluster as soon as temperature is back under control, per your weekly check-in.
- This month, we'll also work on knowing when the crop stops earning its keep: are the yields worth the labor and the propane, and is it worth shortening tomatoes to run a winter of greens. And we'll cover the three ways fall profit gets lost in the last mile: gray mold, silent small fruit, and September root loss.
